What ran

360 fitted/evaluated configurations: 3 datasets × 6 methods × 4 conditions × 5 stratified 75/25 splits. Each final dataset has 800 records (400/class), with 600 training and 200 test records per split. Input is capped at the first 2,000 characters for all methods to bound this CPU pilot. Public source files were downloaded; the synthetic set was generated with known binary labels. All numbers below come from executed predictions.

Clean-data leaders. phishing_text: char_SVM macro-F1 0.947 (split SD 0.012). sms_proxy: char_SVM macro-F1 0.967 (split SD 0.013). synthetic_incidents: word_SVM macro-F1 1.000 (split SD 0.000).

1 · Method recovered from the paper

Worked from the synced 35-page full text, matching title and author Paulo Leocadio, not just its abstract. The file bears Authorea DOI 10.22541/au.177446759.98281679/v1 (2026), while its title-page citation identifies the requested 2025 PRE-PRINT v2 and ResearchGate DOI. Treat this as the available later-hosted version, not a verified byte-identical 2025 original. Web searches located Authorea and ResearchGate, but both full-text HTTP fetches returned 403.

Sections 4.1–4.5 (pp. 8–14) choose distilbert-base-uncased, sequence-classification heads, three training epochs, learning rate 5×10⁻⁵, batch 16, weight decay .01 and up to 512 tokens. The stated data include ~5,000 private SIEM alerts, ~2,000 incident descriptions and 10,000 balanced emails; no underlying records or trained weights are supplied in the inspected materials. The paper specifies 80/10/10 splits in prose but demonstrates a 90/10 training/test example. Here all baselines use the same prespecified 75/25 split, no test-driven tuning.

The paper considers BERT, RoBERTa and SecureBERT alternatives and compares with rules/manual workflows. We add common low-cost text reference methods: majority, keyword rules, TF-IDF multinomial Naive Bayes, logistic regression, word linear SVM and character linear SVM. These additions are our benchmark design, not claimed implementations from the paper. Official Diffusers documentation describes generative diffusion workflows; the paper's own listing imports transformers.

Related author context checked: Differentiable Latent Indexing in LLMs (2026), abstract and opening full-text sections, explicitly says it does not claim empirical validation; Large Language Models: Machine Learning Down to Earth (2023), abstract. Neither supplies incident labels/checkpoints, so neither was used to fill missing experiments.

  • Phishing-labelled text — ealvaradob/phishing-dataset, texts.json, label 1=phishing. 400 samples per class in the final bounded pilot, drawn from an initial stratified pool after deduplication. The repository card declares Apache-2.0 and cites email/SMS sources; constituent rights and per-record provenance remain unaudited. Do not describe it as a verified pure phishing-email benchmark. Text source/style and thread leakage are possible.
  • SMS proxy — UCI SMS Spam Collection, CC BY 4.0. Spam is not phishing. The initial pool retained 1,600 ham plus 622 spam after deduplication; the final pilot samples 400 per class. Its balanced class mix is not deployment prevalence. Actual parsed source rows: 5,574.
  • Synthetic incident diagnostic — 800 descriptions sampled from a 3,200-description generator, benign events versus malicious events, approximately 25% event negation. Ground truth follows the confirmed/ruled-out event category; random host IDs, usernames and priorities are nuisance features. The same small event vocabulary occurs in train/test deliberately: this tests negation composition, not unseen incident generalization. No natural-language validity is claimed.
  • Leakage limits — before sampling real data, collapse whitespace/case and normalize URLs and standalone numbers to identify duplicate groups; discard conflicting-label groups and keep one record per group. Vectorizers fit training only. This blocks those canonical exact duplicates before the character cap, not all near-duplicates, identical capped prefixes, campaign, author, organization or chronological leakage. Datasets are evaluated separately, never pooled or treated as independent replications of the same population.

4 · Difficulty and noise grid

Clean training and clean test; 20% of test whitespace tokens have one character replaced by '*'; test text truncated to the first 40 words; or exactly 15% of training labels flipped without changing test truth. Clean fitted models are reused for the two test-only corruptions; models are refit for training-label noise. Fit times for those reused models report the original clean fit cost. No threshold tuning. Hyperparameters: word TF-IDF 1–2 grams, min_df=2, max_features=18,000; char TF-IDF 3–5 grams, min_df=2, max_features=22,000; sublinear term frequency; NB α=1, LR/SVM C=1. Word and character models differ in both representation and feature cap. The keyword rule flags any of password, verify, urgent, click, account or the synthetic malicious event phrases; it has no learned parameters. It is a transparent diagnostic rule, not a production mail filter.

5 · Statistical comparisons

Prespecified reference: word SVM. Inferential comparisons use only seed 17, avoiding treatment of overlapping repeat splits as independent samples. Exact two-sided McNemar tests compare paired correctness/accuracy, not macro-F1. Holm adjustment covers all 60 comparisons (3 datasets × 4 settings × 5 alternatives). Macro-F1 differences have separate pointwise 95% percentile intervals from 300 class-stratified paired bootstrap draws; these exploratory intervals are not multiplicity-adjusted and assume independent test records. Corruption settings reuse test records and are not independent replications.

Do not infer equivalence from a non-significant test, or use these tests to claim a model beats DistilBERT. Benchmark population validity is limited by source/template correlation.

6 · Failure cases

Measured synthetic failure pattern: word SVM reached mean macro-F1 1.000 on the deliberately shared event vocabulary, versus 0.941 for character SVM. On the clean seed-17 split, all 19 character-SVM errors contained 'ruled out'; word SVM made no errors. Thus the public-text leader was not the best method for this negation diagnostic. This is not evidence of general incident-understanding ability.

Interpretation. Inspect benign messages flagged by credential/action words for costly false positives, and missed malicious messages for absent/obfuscated cues. Synthetic 'ruled out' cases expose whether a classifier handles negation rather than keyword presence; this task intentionally makes that shortcut unreliable. These are candidate mechanisms for manual review, not causally established explanations of every public-data error. Source mixing and label mistakes can also produce apparent failures.

7 · What this does—and does not—support

  • It supplies measured CPU reference points and a stress-test harness for the paper's text-classification component.
  • The source paper reports precision .90, recall .85 and F1 .87 (p.11), with case-study timing and manual-baseline assumptions (pp.17–18). Those are not rerun results and are not ranked against this pilot's different data. Its p.11 statement connecting 90% precision to 10% FPR conflates different denominators.
  • No trained DistilBERT, BERT, RoBERTa or SecureBERT was evaluated. A separately marked optional DistilBERT training script is included but not executed or empirically verified; downloading pretrained weights and CPU fine-tuning were outside this quick run. There is no fabricated transformer score.
  • No SOC action was taken. No manual analyst comparison, NER evaluation, workflow efficacy, MTTR reduction or safe-autoclosure conclusion is supported by text-only classifications.
  • Timing is single-container CPU wall-clock; inference is median of three warmed batch runs. It is not per-request service latency or comparable to the paper's Tesla T4 timing. No memory benchmark was run.

Reproduce and extend

Unzip reproducibility.zip into a local directory. It contains executable scripts, a frozen prepared data sample, all prediction-level outputs and a run manifest. In an environment with the listed baseline packages run OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 benchmark.py, then python3 make_report.py. No network is required with the frozen sample. The optional train_distilbert.py requires torch and transformers in your own environment, uses the same split/corruption functions and 3 fixed epochs without test-set selection. It writes separate prediction files; it does not silently add unrun rows to this leaderboard. Extend all five seeds and compare paired predictions before updating conclusions.
